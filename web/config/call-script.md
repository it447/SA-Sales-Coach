# Call structure

This is the shape a healthy Scale Army intro/discovery call tends to take,
derived from real recorded calls (not a rigid script to read verbatim —
reps phrase things differently and jump around based on what the client
brings up). Judge each phase as covered based on the SUBSTANCE of what was
said, not exact wording, and regardless of what order it happened in.

## 1. Agenda set (`agendaSet`)

The rep told the client how the call will run before diving into
questions — e.g. "I'm going to ask you some questions to understand your
needs, then walk you through our process." Doesn't need to be word-for-word;
any clear framing of what's about to happen counts. On a call that's a
continuation of an existing relationship (no fresh framing needed because
the client already knows the drill), treat this as covered.

## 2. Discovery (`discoveryCovered`)

The rep understood WHY the client is looking to hire now and what their
current situation is — what prompted this, what they're doing today (or
not doing), what's not working, and ideally some sense of urgency or
business impact (e.g. "what would this look like solved in 90 days?").
Covered once the rep has a real picture of the client's current state and
motivation, not just the job title they want filled.

Our actual discovery framework, for judging substance (none of this is a
script to check off word-for-word — the client often volunteers this
without being asked directly, and that counts just as much):

- **Probe** — understanding the current state: "Tell me a little more
  about ___", "Can you walk me through how you're currently handling
  [the function]?"
- **Provoke** — surfacing impact/urgency: "What kind of results are you
  getting with that?", "How does this affect your team/growth?", "How long
  has this been an issue?", a 1-10 self-rating of how well they're handling
  it today plus "why did you give yourself a X?"
- **Desired** — the target state: "If we got that to an 8/9/10, what would
  that look like?", "What business outcomes would this role drive?"
- **Priority/process** — urgency and prior attempts: "How urgent is it to
  get there?", "Have you tried something like this before (hiring it
  yourself, a vendor, Fiverr) — what worked, what didn't?"

Any of these being substantively addressed (rep-asked or client-volunteered)
counts toward `discoveryCovered` — don't require every single one.

## 3. Role scoping

Handled automatically from the structured role data (title, seniority,
region, must-haves) rather than judged here — not part of this tool's
output.

## 4. Consultative diagnosis (`consultativeDiagnosisGiven`)

The rep did more than take an order — they pushed back, clarified, or
proactively flagged something the client hadn't fully thought through.
Examples from real calls: telling a client their one requested role is
actually two different jobs ("this is a unicorn"), pointing out a gap the
client is underestimating ("not having someone on this counts as a revenue
leak"), or recommending a different seniority/structure than what was
asked for. Covered once the rep has added a real opinion or insight, not
just repeated back what the client said.

Also counts: reflecting the client's situation back to confirm understanding
("From my understanding, you're currently X, but you want to get to Y —
does that sound right?"), or a support/decision-process check ("Is there
anyone else you need to loop in to make this decision?") that surfaces a
real complication in how the deal will actually close.

## 5. Process explained (`processExplained`)

The rep explained how Scale Army's process actually works: how candidates
are sourced/vetted (volume, self-reported salary expectations, video
submissions), what the client receives and when (a candidate sheet,
typically in 5-7 business days), the interview/test-assignment step, and
the contract terms (month-to-month, notice period, free replacement
guarantee). Doesn't need every detail — covered once the client has a real
sense of what happens after this call and what they're committing to.

## 6. Pricing discussed (`pricingDiscussed`)

A concrete price range (or the mechanism for one — e.g. "candidates
self-report, we filter by your max") was discussed for at least one role,
even if it wasn't precise or didn't result in a locked number.

## Preempting objections before they come up

Beyond reacting to pushback (see scoping-rules.md's objection-handling
playbook), a strong rep also proactively shuts doors that would otherwise
become an objection later — e.g. asking about urgency ("How urgent is it
to get there — fixing this yesterday, in 2 weeks, or in 3 months?") and
prior attempts ("Have you tried something like this before — what worked,
what didn't?") surfaces timeline and budget objections early, while
they're still easy to address. Treat this the same as consultative
diagnosis above when judging that phase — it's still the rep adding real
insight, not just taking an order.

## 7. Close attempted (`closeAttempted`)

The rep asked for something concrete to move forward — a deposit, a
signature, scheduling the next call, or an explicit next step with a
timeline. A call that just trails off with no ask does NOT count, even if
the conversation was otherwise thorough.
